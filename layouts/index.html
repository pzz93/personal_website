Biography

Welcome! I am an Assistant Professor at the Central University of Finance and Economics. I received my Ph.D. in Finance from Nanyang Technological University in 2023.

My research examines how investors form beliefs and how financial information is produced and communicated. I am particularly interested in why investors favor certain securities, how professional analysts and individual investors develop and share investment ideas, and how these ideas influence trading behavior and asset prices. My work spans behavioral finance, empirical asset pricing, and empirical corporate finance.

Methodologically, I work with large-scale, granular financial data, with a particular focus on unstructured information. I use textual analysis, train and fine-tune task-specific NLP models, and apply large language models (LLMs) to measure investor beliefs and extract economically meaningful information that is difficult to observe in conventional financial datasets.

Publication

The Review of Financial Studies 2025

Why Do Investors Like Short-leg Securities? Evidence from a Textual Analysis of Buy Recommendations

Hailiang Chen, Byoung-Hyoun Hwang, and Zhuozhen Peng

The Review of Financial Studies, 38(12), 3729–3767 (2025).

Journal Article ↗
Abstract

Our paper examines analyst reports and online stock opinion articles, which recommend investors buy stocks that, based on prior literature, trade at comparatively high prices and earn low future returns (“short-leg securities”). We conduct textual analysis and test whether the justifications provided in these buy recommendations primarily (1) emphasize a stock’s safe-haven quality, (2) indicate general investor exuberance, or (3) highlight a stock’s lottery-like features. We find that the buy recommendations mostly emphasize stocks’ lottery-like characteristics. We subsequently validate our text-based inferences through a one-time survey of actual institutional and retail investors with long positions in short-leg securities. Overall, our results suggest that perceived lottery-like features play a material role in explaining why investors like and invest in stocks that reside in short legs of anomalies.
